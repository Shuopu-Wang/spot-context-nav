How a study runs

  1. Upload training images under Training images. Open each one, place the robot's start and a goal flag, then choose Draw paths and draw as many candidate paths as you like.
  2. Share the team link with your team and the survey link with everyone else. Team members rank the paths on every photo; survey participants on 5 random ones. Only photos with at least one path are shown.
  3. Participants rank each photo's paths from best to worst and flag any that aren't socially acceptable. Each photo's folder gets the scores, the best-ranked path and every ranking.
  4. Optional: upload images your VLM drew under VLM images; each person then rates 10 of them at the end. Set that number to 0 under Settings to skip it.
  5. Compare models under Results and download everything under Data.

Results

How people rated each model's paths, and, for paths with coordinates (traced or uploaded as JSON), how closely they match what people drew.

Raters

Questionnaire, mean per model

Each VLM image counts once (the mean of its ratings). Brackets: 95% bootstrap interval over images. Last row: Krippendorff's alpha, how consistently different raters answered (1 = perfect agreement, 0 = chance; above about 0.67 is usually treated as acceptable).

Scale answers at a glance

Dot = mean, line = 95% interval. Labels name the model.

Path agreement with people

Only VLM outputs with coordinates. Trace a VLM image's path on its detail page to include it.

Same route as people
Share of human responses that chose the same route (or also waited). 95% bootstrap interval in brackets.
Fréchet to compiled path
Largest gap from the compiled human trajectory, as % of the image diagonal. Lower is better.
Similarity
1 / (1 + Fréchet ÷ same-route threshold): 100% identical, 50% right at the same-route threshold.
Human percentile
How typical the path is among people: 50% = as close to the crowd as an average person.

VLM paths as coordinates

If your pipeline outputs coordinates, upload them as JSON instead of (or as well as) images: they're compared with human paths directly, and drawn as images for rating. Points are normalized image coordinates [x, y], 0–1 from the top-left.

[{"scene_title": "welch crowd", "model": "gpt-4o-fewshot",
  "decision": "path", "points": [[0.5,0.96],[0.38,0.6],[0.52,0.3]],
  "style": "curve", "confidence": 0.7, "norms": ["terrain"],
  "rationale": "Grass is empty; sidewalk is blocked."}]
